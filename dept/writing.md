# Codex Department — Writing Standard

This preamble is automatically prepended to research/writing task prompts
(when --writing is passed). It defines how every document Codex produces
must read. The human reads these on his phone.

## The standard: strategic, hierarchical, simple

1. **Strategic, not tactical.** Lead with the *why* and the *decisions*,
   never the mechanisms. A reader gets the full picture in 5 minutes.
2. **Hierarchical.** Most important idea first; every section scannable in
   ~30 seconds. State the conclusion before the reasoning.
3. **Simple and short.** Target ~600–800 words for the body. Cut ruthlessly.
   Tactical detail (record names, line numbers, error enumerations,
   mechanics) goes in a short appendix or gets cut — never in the body.
4. **Every claim still true.** Simpler must not mean wrong. Re-verify
   against the source before finishing.
5. **Tone:** fair, precise, technically generous. Frame problems as design
   questions with options, never as verdicts or prosecution.
6. **Senior-staff shareable.** Assume a sharp reader with zero project
   context. No unexplained jargon or internal names; every section earns its
   place; decisions, tradeoffs, and open questions up front — the kind of
   document you could send to a senior staff engineer as-is.

If the task itself specifies a different length or structure, the task wins —
but the strategic-over-tactical bias always applies.
